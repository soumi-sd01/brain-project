# Dataset Selection Justification
## Research Project: Conformal Calibration Under Missing MRI Modalities

### 1. Introduction

The objective of this research project is to investigate the reliability of brain tumour segmentation models when one or more MRI modalities are unavailable. The study aims to compare ordinary conformal calibration with modality-conditional conformal calibration under different missing-modality scenarios.

To conduct these experiments, we require a dataset that provides:

- Multiple MRI modalities for each patient.
- Expert-annotated brain tumour segmentation masks.
- Sufficient patient-level samples for training, validation, calibration and testing.
- Consistent imaging and annotation formats.
- A recognised benchmark suitable for reproducible medical imaging research.

The BraTS 2023 Adult Glioma (BraTS-GLI) dataset has been selected as the proposed dataset for this research.

### 2. What is BraTS?

BraTS stands for Brain Tumor Segmentation Challenge. It is an established research benchmark designed to support the development and evaluation of automated brain tumour segmentation methods using MRI scans.

The dataset provides multimodal brain MRI examinations together with corresponding tumour segmentation annotations.

It is particularly relevant to this project because it allows researchers to investigate how different MRI sequences contribute to tumour segmentation and how segmentation performance changes when individual modalities are unavailable.

### 3. Why Four MRI Modalities Are Important

BraTS-GLI provides four complementary MRI sequences:

| Modality | Description |
|---|---|
| T1 (T1n) | T1-weighted anatomical imaging |
| T1ce (T1c) | Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging |
| T2 (T2w) | T2-weighted imaging |
| FLAIR (T2f) | Fluid-attenuated inversion recovery imaging |

Each modality provides different information about brain anatomy and tumour characteristics.

The availability of these four sequences is essential for our research because we intend to simulate situations in which one or more modalities are missing.

For example:

- Complete input: T1 + T1ce + T2 + FLAIR
- Missing T1: T1ce + T2 + FLAIR
- Missing T1ce: T1 + T2 + FLAIR
- Missing T2: T1 + T1ce + FLAIR
- Missing FLAIR: T1 + T1ce + T2
- Multiple missing modalities: Different combinations of the available sequences.

These scenarios will allow us to systematically investigate the effect of modality absence on segmentation accuracy and prediction reliability.

### 4. Availability of Expert Segmentation Annotations

Another important reason for selecting BraTS is that it provides corresponding tumour segmentation masks.

These annotations identify different tumour regions:

- NCR/NET: Necrotic tumour core and non-enhancing tumour.
- ED: Peritumoral oedema.
- ET: Enhancing tumour.

These labels can be combined to evaluate three commonly used tumour regions:

- Whole Tumour (WT).
- Tumour Core (TC).
- Enhancing Tumour (ET).

The segmentation masks provide the ground truth required to evaluate the predictions generated by our 3D U-Net model.

We can use these annotations to calculate segmentation metrics such as Dice Similarity Coefficient and Intersection over Union.

They also provide the reference information necessary for evaluating conformal prediction coverage.

### 5. Why BraTS 2023 Adult Glioma Specifically?

BraTS has multiple historical releases, including BraTS 2021 and BraTS 2023.

BraTS 2023 Adult Glioma (BraTS-GLI) is proposed for this project for the following reasons:

**5.1 Practical dataset accessibility**

The historical BraTS 2018–2021 datasets are no longer hosted through their original Synapse access route. BraTS 2023 GLI provides a current official access route through Synapse, subject to registration and acceptance of applicable data-use terms.

**5.2 Multimodal MRI availability**

The dataset contains the four MRI modalities required for our missing-modality experiments.

**5.3 Segmentation ground truth**

The corresponding tumour segmentation annotations allow us to evaluate segmentation performance and prediction reliability.

**5.4 Established research benchmark**

BraTS is widely used in brain tumour segmentation research, making it an appropriate benchmark for a reproducible research prototype.

**5.5 Compatibility with controlled modality removal**

We can artificially remove selected MRI modalities while preserving the corresponding patient-level segmentation annotations. This enables controlled experiments involving different modality availability patterns.

**Important annotation consideration:**

The enhancing tumour label convention differs between releases:

- BraTS 2021: Enhancing tumour label = 4.
- BraTS 2023 GLI: Enhancing tumour label = 3.

Therefore, preprocessing and label interpretation must follow the actual dataset release. Labels from different releases must not be mixed without explicit harmonisation.

### 6. Why Not Use Other MRI Datasets?

We considered the general characteristics of alternative medical imaging datasets.

| Dataset | Multimodal MRI | Tumour Segmentation | Suitability |
|---|---|---|---|
| BraTS | Yes | Yes | Excellent |
| Medical Segmentation Decathlon Task 1 | Yes | Yes | Good |
| IXI | Yes | Not equivalent to glioma segmentation annotations | Limited |
| General brain MRI collections | Varies | Varies | Depends on annotations |

BraTS is particularly suitable because it provides paired multimodal MRI scans and corresponding segmentation masks.

This reduces the time required for dataset preparation and allows us to concentrate on the main research objectives.

### 7. Relevance to Conformal Prediction

The primary objective of this research is not simply to improve segmentation accuracy. It is to investigate prediction reliability under missing-modality conditions.

Conformal prediction provides a statistical framework for constructing prediction sets with a target coverage level under appropriate assumptions.

For example, with a significance level of alpha = 0.10, the target marginal coverage is:

1 - alpha = 0.90, or 90%.

We intend to investigate two calibration approaches.

**7.1 Ordinary Conformal Calibration**

A common calibration threshold is estimated using a held-out calibration dataset.

The same threshold is then applied to test examples, regardless of their modality availability pattern.

**7.2 Modality-Conditional Conformal Calibration**

Calibration examples are grouped according to their available MRI modalities.

Separate calibration thresholds are estimated for different modality availability groups, provided that sufficient calibration samples are available.

For example:

- All four modalities available.
- T1 missing.
- T1ce missing.
- T2 missing.
- FLAIR missing.
- Multiple modalities missing.

The two approaches will be compared using:

- Overall prediction coverage.
- Coverage across missing-modality groups.
- Prediction-set size.
- Prediction-set efficiency.
- Segmentation performance.

**Statistical consideration:** Ordinary split conformal prediction provides marginal coverage under exchangeability assumptions. Modality-specific calibration may improve groupwise coverage, but separate thresholds do not automatically guarantee exact conditional coverage. Calibration sample sizes and statistical assumptions must be considered when interpreting the results.

### 8. Proposed Experimental Workflow

The selected dataset will be used in the following experimental pipeline:

1. Download the official BraTS-GLI dataset.
2. Inspect the actual directory structure and image files.
3. Verify the four MRI modalities and segmentation annotations.
4. Perform patient-level training, validation, calibration and test splitting.
5. Preprocess the MRI volumes and segmentation masks.
6. Develop a 3D U-Net segmentation model.
7. Introduce modality dropout during training.
8. Construct controlled missing-modality evaluation scenarios.
9. Implement ordinary conformal calibration.
10. Implement modality-conditional conformal calibration.
11. Evaluate coverage, prediction-set efficiency and segmentation performance.
12. Compare the experimental findings.

No patient should appear in more than one data partition.

### 9. Dataset Limitations

Although BraTS is appropriate for this research, certain limitations must be acknowledged.

**9.1 Dataset generalisation**

BraTS is a research benchmark and may not fully represent the diversity of MRI acquisition protocols and patient populations encountered in clinical practice.

**9.2 Synthetic missing modalities**

Our experiments will simulate missing modalities by deliberately removing MRI sequences. This does not represent every real-world reason for modality unavailability.

**9.3 Calibration sample size**

Some modality availability groups may contain relatively few calibration patients. This can lead to unstable calibration thresholds and unreliable groupwise estimates.

**9.4 Computational requirements**

Three-dimensional MRI volumes require substantial memory and computational resources. Preprocessing, patch-based training or controlled downsampling may be necessary when using Google Colab with a limited GPU.

**9.5 Distribution shift**

Conformal coverage guarantees rely on assumptions about the relationship between calibration and test data. Significant distribution differences may invalidate the expected coverage properties.

### 10. Final Justification

The BraTS 2023 Adult Glioma dataset was selected as the proposed dataset for this study because it provides multimodal brain MRI examinations comprising T1-weighted, post-contrast T1-weighted, T2-weighted and FLAIR sequences, together with expert-annotated tumour segmentation masks. The availability of multiple complementary imaging modalities makes the dataset appropriate for investigating segmentation performance under controlled modality-absence scenarios. Furthermore, the voxel-level reference annotations enable quantitative evaluation of segmentation accuracy and conformal prediction coverage. The dataset therefore provides a suitable experimental foundation for comparing ordinary conformal calibration with modality-conditional conformal calibration. Synthetic modality removal will be used to construct controlled missing-modality scenarios, with patient-level separation between training, validation, calibration and test partitions.
